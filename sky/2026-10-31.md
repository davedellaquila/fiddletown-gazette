**Tonight's chart (evening, ~8 PM, facing south):** the Great Square of Pegasus rides high overhead; Cassiopeia climbs in the northeast; the Summer Triangle (Vega, Deneb, Altair) sinks in the west; Fomalhaut glows low in the south; **Saturn** shines steady in the south in Cetus. Orion rises in the east late in the evening. Early evening is moonless — the Moon doesn't rise until around 11 PM.

**Moon:** Waning gibbous, about two-thirds lit (65%), five nights past the Hunter's Moon full moon (Oct 26). Rises late (~11 PM), so the early evening is properly dark. Last quarter tomorrow, Nov 1 — the same night the clocks go back.

**Planets up:**
- **Saturn** (mag ~0.5, in Cetus) — the evening planet: already up as darkness falls (~6:00 PM sunset), well placed all evening in the south. Its rings are nearly edge-on this season; any small telescope shows them.
- **Jupiter** (mag −2.0, in Leo) — rises late; brilliant before dawn, lined up with **Mars** (mag ~0.9, reddish) and the star Regulus. Late tonight the waning Moon passes near Pollux.

**Notable events:** Two meteor showers are active — the **Orionids** (past their Oct 21–22 peak, winding down through Nov 7) and the **Taurids**, the slow bright "Halloween fireballs" (Southern Taurids peak Nov 4–5). A few an hour at most, but Taurids can be spectacular.

**Viewing note (honest):** tonight's forecast is rain and cloud over the foothills, so most of this is likely hidden — the chart is what's up there when the clouds part. The week ahead clears (Sun–Mon mostly sunny), and the moonless early evenings early in the week are the better bet.
