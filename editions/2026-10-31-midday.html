📜 This Day in History — Oct 31

⭐ From our own hills — living history, today: the Chew Kee Museum in Fiddletown, an 1850s rammed-earth Chinese herb shop from the town's Gold Rush days, closes its season today — last chance to step inside until spring (12:00–4:00 PM).

1850 — William Daylor dies on this day. Daylor, partner of Jared Sheldon, discovered the Cosumnes River Valley — our river: the canal diverting the Cosumnes, finished in 1853, brought water to Fiddletown's dry diggings and made the town boom.

1849 — the bark Ark sails for the gold fields from Newburyport with 117 passengers; 205 days around Cape Horn later she reaches San Francisco — the kind of voyage that filled the Mother Lode camps, ours included.

1850 — the U.S. Census is taken in Placerville on Oct 31, counting miners and traders in the diggings a day's ride north — the Gold Rush, enumerated.

1864 — Nevada becomes the 36th state. Amador still celebrates Nevada Day: the winery specials across the Shenandoah Valley this weekend are a 160-year-old tradition.

Wider page: 1913 the Lincoln Highway is dedicated · 1926 Harry Houdini dies · 1941 Mount Rushmore is completed.

The editor dug back to 1848 for this date in our hills, as the publisher directed: no verified Fiddletown event falls on Oct 31 in the Gold Rush years — the finds above are our watershed and our Mother Lode. Sources: Fiddletown Preservation Society · Elk Grove Historical Society · Maritime Heritage Project · Gardner Library · Britannica

🔭 Star Chart — Tonight over Fiddletown

Tonight's chart (~8 PM, facing south): the Great Square of Pegasus rides high overhead, Cassiopeia climbs in the northeast, the Summer Triangle sinks in the west, and Saturn shines steady in the south in Cetus. Orion rises in the east late in the evening. Early evening is moonless — the Moon doesn't rise until around 11 PM.

🌖 Moon: waning gibbous, about two-thirds lit (65%), five nights past the Hunter's Moon. Last quarter tomorrow, Nov 1 — the same night the clocks go back.

Planets up: Saturn (mag ~0.5) takes the evening — up as darkness falls (~6:00 PM sunset), well placed all evening; its rings are nearly edge-on this season. Jupiter (mag −2.0) and reddish Mars rise late in Leo, lined up with Regulus, brilliant before dawn.

Notable: two meteor showers are active — the Orionids, winding down, and the Taurids, the slow bright “Halloween fireballs” (Southern Taurids peak Nov 4–5).

Viewing note: honestly, tonight's rain and cloud will likely hide most of this — this is what's up there when the clouds part. The clearing, moonless early evenings early in the week are the better bet. Dark-sky country is part of foothill life; this box treats it that way.

Sources: TheSkyLive · StarWalk / Sky Tonight · Astronomy Magazine · NWS Sacramento (conditions) · Checked Oct 31

🐦 On the Wing

THE GAZETTE'S BIRD-WATCHING DESK — FIRST SIGHTING

Northern Flicker, red-shafted form — a male. Filmed from a Fiddletown deck on Halloween morning, hitching up a trunk in a tree gone full fall red, pines behind. Watch for the field marks: the brown back barred with black, the spotted cream breast, the gray face — and, because he's a he, the red mustache stripe.

54 seconds · Filmed by the publisher, Fiddletown, Halloween morning

The flicker is the West's own: in flight, the underwings and tail flash salmon-red — that's the “red-shafted” part. One of our larger woodpeckers at about 11 inches long with a 17-inch wingspan, and a year-round resident of Amador County's foothill woodland.

He's also the family oddball: flickers spend much of their lives on the ground, hunting ants — they eat more ants than almost any other North American bird. Up a trunk, as here, he braces with that stiff tail and probes the bark. Listen for him before you see him: a loud rolling wick-a-wick-a-wick-a, and a sharp klear!

🦝 The Night Shift, Caught by the AstroTurf

SIGHTING No. 1 FOR THIS DESK · SAT OCT 31, 2026 · FILMED BY DAVE DELL'AQUILA, FIDDLETOWN

The desk opens its books with the neighborhood's night shift: raccoons — a family party, at least two and likely three — working the garden bed by the AstroTurf in the small hours. Grizzled backs down, noses in the planting bed by the white garden flower, prospecting for grubs, insects, and whatever the garden gives up in late October; one sits up at the bed's edge to survey the water, then the whole crew carries on into the dark, unhurried.

Raccoon kits travel with their mother well into fall, which is what this looks like: a family working a route together. Masked, ring-tailed, and equipped with the cleverest hands in the neighborhood, autumn raccoons are eating for winter — admire from the deck, secure the pet food, and give the night shift its room.

Correction, Oct 31: this desk first named the visitor a striped skunk — a grizzled back in two tight frames read as a skunk's stripe. The frame-by-frame recheck (and the publisher's own eye) found no white stripe anywhere, and more than one animal. The record stands corrected, and the desk thanks its readers who catch what it misses.

🚜 What's Carl Working On

The saga of the couch rolls on another week —
it's listed, it's measured, the outlook is bleak.
It promised to go, but it's still sitting tight;
Carl's verdict (print edition): “a pain” — and he's right.

The tractor, meanwhile, got shown proper care:
new filter, greased pins, every bolt checked with care.
The brush hog's swapped out for the box blade's turn,
before rain makes the pasture too soft to churn.

The burn pile stands stacked — tall, tidy, and neat —
awaiting its burn day; it won't cheat the heat.
Fresh gravel is down on the range's long lane,
new steel at a hundred yards waits there for aim.

The scooter stands ready for one more warm run —
a last little loop in the late-season sun.
The Vidmar's drawers gleam, every tool in its row,
each socket and wrench in the place it should go.
The canoe hangs up high in the barn, dry and light,
dreaming of spring water, smooth and bright.

So all of the ranch is set right for the snow —
except for that couch, which refuses to go.
And Carl's one demand, by text, has come in:
print scooter, Vidmar, and canoe — he's locked in!

🏆 The Winsteds' Bocce Court

Every neighborhood has a capital, and around here it's the Winsteds' bocce court. Carol keeps the bracket, the scorebook, and — when a throw lands close enough to argue about — the final word. Joe tends the coals, because the court comes with the other half of the institution: the fabulous barbecue, the kind where the brisket has a schedule, the sides appear like magic, and nobody has ever left hungry or entirely sure how the last game ended.

House rules, as best the Gazette can establish them: winners eat first, losers wash up, and a ball within a finger's width of the pallino is settled by Carol — appeal is not a thing.

🍞 Nan's Recipe Corner

Bourbon Maple Old Fashioned — Nan's second filing, and a change of pace from the bake: a five-minute cocktail with an autumn accent, bourbon warmed up with maple. Makes 1 cocktail.

By Nan Dell'Aquila · Filed by email, Oct 31

In the shaker: 2 oz bourbon · ½ oz maple syrup · 2 dashes orange bitters (or a combination of cherry bitters and chile bitters) · an orange twist, to garnish

Fill a shaker with ice and add the bourbon, maple syrup, and bitters; shake twenty times to combine and chill. Strain over one large ice cube in an Old-Fashioned glass and finish with the orange twist. Five minutes, start to sip.
