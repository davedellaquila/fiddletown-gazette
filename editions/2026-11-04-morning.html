📜 This Day in History — Nov 4

⭐ From our own hills' generation — a Gold Rush Californian on the national ballot, 1856: on this day, the presidential election of Tuesday, Nov 4, 1856 puts a Californian on a major-party ticket for the first time: John C. Frémont — explorer of the Sierra crossings, a Bear Flag figure, and California's first U.S. Senator (seated days after statehood in 1850) — runs as the new Republican Party's first presidential nominee. Democrat James Buchanan wins the day (174 electoral votes to Frémont's 114, with Know-Nothing Millard Fillmore at 8), but the date marks the Gold Rush state's arrival: six years after statehood, California is already supplying national candidates.

1924 — California legalizes professional boxing. Voters pass a measure ending the state's ban on pro boxing, in place since 1914 over safety concerns. The same day, Miriam “Ma” Ferguson of Texas and Nellie Tayloe Ross of Wyoming become the first women elected governor in the United States.

Nov 4 is a California-flavored ballot date: Sen. Richard M. Nixon of California was elected vice president on this day in 1952; former California governor Ronald Reagan was elected president in 1980; and in 2008, Barack Obama was elected the 44th president — the first African American elected to the White House — the same day California voters approved Proposition 8.

Wider page: 1842 Abraham Lincoln marries Mary Todd in Springfield, Illinois · 1922 Howard Carter's team finds the first step of the staircase to Tutankhamun's tomb · 1979 the Iran hostage crisis begins as the U.S. embassy in Tehran is stormed.

The editor dug back to 1848 for this date in our hills, as the publisher directed: no verified Fiddletown or Amador event falls on Nov 4 in the Gold Rush years — today's highlight is California's Gold Rush generation on the national stage, and the box says so plainly. Sources: U.S. Senate — California Timeline · Wikipedia, 1856 United States presidential election · HISTORY, Today in History — Nov 4

🔭 Star Chart — Tonight over Fiddletown

Tonight's chart (~8 PM, facing south): Saturn owns the evening — about 46° up in the southeast at 8 PM and peaking at 53° around 9:43 PM. The Great Square of Pegasus rides high overhead, Cassiopeia climbs in the northeast, and Taurus — tonight's meteor radiant — rises in the east through the evening. The evening is moonless — the Moon doesn't rise until 2:59 AM.

🌒 Moon: waning crescent, about one-sixth lit (~17%) — past last quarter (Nov 1) and thinning toward the Nov 9 new moon. It sets at 2:33 PM today and rises at 2:59 AM, so the whole evening is properly dark.

Planets up: Saturn takes the evening — high in the south all evening, setting just before 4 AM. Mars rises about 11:40 PM and Jupiter just after midnight, both brilliant before dawn. Venus is a pre-dawn object only, rising about 5:05 AM. Mercury is below the horizon at sunset — not an evening target tonight.

Notable — the Southern Taurids PEAK tonight, Nov 4–5: the slow, bright “fireballs” from Comet 2P/Encke's debris. Rates are modest — about 5–10 meteors an hour under dark skies, per the American Meteor Society — but the stream's larger particles make occasional fireballs brighter than Venus. No fireball swarm is predicted this year (the next is expected in 2032); the best hours are around and after midnight, when Taurus rides high. The Northern Taurids follow Nov 11–12, and the Orionids wind down through Nov 7.

Viewing note (honest): a work-the-gaps night, not a clear one. NWS calls tonight mostly cloudy (low around 45°), and the hourly picture says the early evening is the best window — mostly clear about 7 PM, partly cloudy at 8 PM, which is Saturn's window — before cloud builds from about 9 PM; the Taurid peak hours after midnight run mostly cloudy, easing to partly cloudy around 2–3 AM. The Moon stays down until 2:59 AM, so the sky between clouds is properly dark, and Taurid fireballs are bright enough to show through gaps and thin cloud. Plan: Saturn early, fireball watch in the gaps late, dress warm.

🚜 What's Carl Working On

The saga of the couch rolls on another week —
it's listed, it's measured, the outlook is bleak.
It promised to go, but it's still sitting tight;
Carl's verdict (print edition): “a pain” — and he's right.

The tractor, meanwhile, got shown proper care:
new filter, greased pins, every bolt checked with care.
The brush hog's swapped out for the box blade's turn,
before rain makes the pasture too soft to churn.

The burn pile stands stacked — tall, tidy, and neat —
awaiting its burn day; it won't cheat the heat.
Fresh gravel is down on the range's long lane,
new steel at a hundred yards waits there for aim.

The scooter stands ready for one more warm run —
a last little loop in the late-season sun.
The Vidmar's drawers gleam, every tool in its row,
each socket and wrench in the place it should go.
The canoe hangs up high in the barn, dry and light,
dreaming of spring water, smooth and bright.

So all of the ranch is set right for the snow —
except for that couch, which refuses to go.
And Carl's one demand, by text, has come in:
print scooter, Vidmar, and canoe — he's locked in!

🏆 The Winsteds' Bocce Court

Every neighborhood has a capital, and around here it's the Winsteds' bocce court. Carol keeps the bracket, the scorebook, and — when a throw lands close enough to argue about — the final word. Joe tends the coals, because the court comes with the other half of the institution: the fabulous barbecue, the kind where the brisket has a schedule, the sides appear like magic, and nobody has ever left hungry or entirely sure how the last game ended.

House rules, as best the Gazette can establish them: winners eat first, losers wash up, and a ball within a finger's width of the pallino is settled by Carol — appeal is not a thing.

🍞 Nan's Recipe Corner

Chicken Parmesan Rolls — Nan's Sunday filing, and a return to the oven: chicken cutlets rolled around mozzarella and basil, crumbed, baked in marinara, and finished under more mozzarella. Serves 4.

By Nan Dell'Aquila · Filed by email, Nov 1

In the dish: 2 cups marinara sauce (from a 24-oz. jar, such as Rao's), divided · 4 boneless, skinless chicken breasts (8 oz. each) · 2 tsp. kosher salt · 1 tsp. black pepper, plus more to garnish · 1 (16-oz.) pkg. sliced fresh mozzarella (16 slices) · 2 Tbsp. chopped fresh basil, plus torn leaves to garnish · 6 Tbsp. finely shredded Parmigiano-Reggiano, divided · 2 large eggs, lightly beaten · 1 cup seasoned Italian breadcrumbs · cooking spray

Heat the oven to 450°F and spread 1 cup of the marinara in a 13- x 9-inch baking dish. Halve the chicken breasts horizontally into 8 cutlets, pound them to an even ¼ inch, and season all over with the salt and pepper. Crumble 8 of the mozzarella slices over the cutlets and scatter on the basil and 4 tablespoons of the Parmigiano-Reggiano; roll each cutlet up jellyroll-style from a short side, tucking in the edges. Dip the rolls in the beaten egg, then coat them in the breadcrumbs mixed with the remaining 2 tablespoons of cheese, pressing lightly. Set them seam-side down in the dish, spray lightly, and bake until a thermometer reads 165°F — 20 to 25 minutes. Spoon the remaining cup of marinara over the top, lay on the last 8 mozzarella slices, and bake 3 to 5 minutes more, until the cheese melts and the sauce is hot. Garnish with basil leaves and an extra turn of pepper.

Nan's note, from her card: she made this on October 21 and reports it delicious and easy — a good one for a small group, served over pasta, with extra sauce for the pasta.

Nan's Halloween-night note, added Nov 1 at her request: “We had a wonderful Halloween night hunkered in with Rachel and Robin. We toasted to the evening with a Bourbon Maple Old Fashioned (yum!), watched a scary movie and made this delicious version of chicken parm. It was easy to partially prepare ahead of time, making the chicken rolls and keeping them in the fridge. When I was ready to cook them I dipped them in the egg and breadcrumbs and followed the final steps before baking them. It is a great meal to make for a group and tasted absolutely delicious 😋”
