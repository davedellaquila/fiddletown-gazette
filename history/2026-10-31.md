- **1864** — Nevada becomes the 36th state, admitted by President Lincoln's proclamation in the middle of the Civil War. The Silver State was born of the Comstock rush — the same mining fever, just over the Sierra, that had filled Gold Country camps like Fiddletown fifteen years earlier.
- **1913** — The Lincoln Highway is dedicated: the first automobile road across America, running from Times Square in New York to Lincoln Park in San Francisco. Towns along its California stretch marked the day with bonfires and parades — the auto age had officially reached the West.
- **1926** — Harry Houdini dies in Detroit at 52, of peritonitis from a ruptured appendix, days after taking blows to the stomach from a student testing his famous claim. The great escape artist's death on Halloween has kept the legend — and the séances — alive ever since.
- **1941** — Work ends on Mount Rushmore after fourteen years. Sculptor Gutzon Borglum had died that spring; his son Lincoln carried the carving to its finish, when the money ran out, on October 31.
- **1994** — Death Valley becomes a national park, as the California Desert Protection Act turns the monument into Death Valley National Park — home to Badwater Basin, the lowest point in North America.
