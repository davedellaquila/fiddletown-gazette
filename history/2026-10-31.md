## HIGHLIGHT — living history, today in Fiddletown
- **Chew Kee Museum, Fiddletown — final day of the season TODAY (12:00–4:00 PM).** One of California's most authentic Gold Rush-era Chinese heritage sites: an 1850s rammed-earth herb shop from Fiddletown's Chinese community (2,000+ residents in the 1850s–60s). Open April 4–October 31; today is the last chance until spring. Source: Fiddletown Preservation Society newsletter.
## Gold Rush-era, this date
- **1850 — William Daylor dies (Oct 31).** Daylor, partner of Jared Sheldon, discovered the Cosumnes River Valley — OUR river: three years later the 22-mile canal diverting the Cosumnes (completed 1853) brought water to Fiddletown's dry diggings and made the town boom. Source: Elk Grove Historical Society timeline.
- **1849 — the bark *Ark* sails for the gold fields (Oct 31)** from Newburyport with 117 passengers; 205 days around Cape Horn, arriving San Francisco May 25, 1850 — the voyage that fed the camps all up and down the Mother Lode, ours included. Source: Maritime Heritage Project.
- **1850 — the U.S. Census is taken in Placerville on Oct 31**, counting miners and "traders" in the diggings — the Gold Rush being enumerated, one camp at a time, a day's ride north of Fiddletown. Source: Gardner Library / Carlisle '49er papers.
## Wider page
- **1864 — Nevada becomes the 36th state** (Oct 31). The local tie is alive: Amador still celebrates Nevada Day — winery specials across the Shenandoah Valley this very weekend are a 160-year-old tradition of toasting the Silver State.
- **1913** — Lincoln Highway dedicated (first transcontinental highway; its Sierra route ran through Placerville, north of us).
- **1926** — Harry Houdini dies in Detroit. **1941** — Mount Rushmore completed, 14 years after carving began.
