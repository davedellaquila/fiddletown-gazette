# This Day in History — Sun Nov 1
Researched Nov 1, 2026 (Morning Edition). Dave's rule: dig back to 1848 for local significance on this date, local → watershed/Mother Lode → California → national, highlight the local/regional find in the gold panel, honest when no in-beat item exists.

## HIGHLIGHT — watershed / Mother Lode gateway, Gold Rush year one
- **1848 — John Sutter Jr. announces plans for Sacramento City (Nov 1).** At the junction of the American River and the Sacramento River — the embarcadero every Mother Lode camp, Fiddletown included, was supplied through. Announced the same year gold was found at Sutter's Mill up the American River; within months this planned town is the gateway through which the forty-niners pour into our hills. Source: This Week in California History (California history timeline, Oct 27–Nov 3).
- Honest note for print: no verified event in Fiddletown/Amador itself falls on Nov 1 in 1848–1860s — the highlight is our watershed and supply gateway, and the box says so.

## California page
- **1776 — Mission San Juan Capistrano dedicated (Nov 1)** by Father Junípero Serra — the 7th of Alta California's 21 missions, famous for its 1812 earthquake ruins and the swallows that return each March. Source: This Week in California History; Almanack On This Day.
- **1919 — California ratifies the 19th Amendment (Nov 1)** — the 18th state to ratify, putting the national campaign halfway to the 36 states needed; California women had already won the vote in state elections in 1911. Source: CalExplornia, California History in November.
- **1910 — San Diego's City Park becomes Balboa Park (Nov 1)**, renamed for Vasco Núñez de Balboa as the city prepared for the Panama-California Exposition. Source: CalExplornia.

## Wider page
- **1512** — Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel ceiling is first shown to the public (Nov 1, All Saints' Day).
- **1938** — Seabiscuit beats War Admiral in the Pimlico match race (Nov 1) — the California-raced underdog horse of the Depression years.
