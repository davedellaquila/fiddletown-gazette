## HIGHLIGHT — from our own hills (gold panel)
- **1849 — Bayard Taylor walks into Drytown (Nov 15), 177 years ago today.** Bayard Taylor, the New York Tribune correspondent touring the gold country for what became *Eldorado; or, Adventures in the Path of Empire* (1850), walked into Drytown — Amador County's oldest town, where mining began on Dry Creek in the spring of 1848 — on November 15, 1849, and found between two and three hundred miners settled in for the winter, the diggings going briskly. A genuine in-beat Gold Rush item on this exact date — the find yesterday's box flagged as "tomorrow's date" lands today. ## National page
- **1777 — The Articles of Confederation are approved (Nov 15)** by the Continental Congress and sent to the states for ratification — the young republic's first frame of government, four years before it took full effect.
- **1864 — Sherman begins the March to the Sea (Nov 15).** Union General William Tecumseh Sherman leaves a burning Atlanta and marches toward Savannah, Georgia, in the American Civil War's most famous scorched-earth campaign.
- **1867 — The first practical stock ticker is introduced in New York City (Nov 15)** — telegraph-fed prices on paper tape, revolutionizing how investors received market information.
- **1926 — NBC launches its national radio network (Nov 15)** — the National Broadcasting Company signs on with a four-hour broadcast from New York, the start of American network radio.
- **1971 — Intel introduces the 4004 (Nov 15)** — the first commercially available single-chip microprocessor, announced in *Electronic News*; designed by Federico Faggin, Ted Hoff, Stan Mazor and Masatoshi Shima for a Busicom calculator, it opened the personal-computing era in the Santa Clara valley country south of the foothills.
