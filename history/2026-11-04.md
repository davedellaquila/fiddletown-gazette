## HIGHLIGHT — California / Gold Rush era (statewide, honestly labeled)
- **1856 — A Gold Rush Californian on the national ballot (Nov 4).** The presidential election of Tuesday, Nov 4, 1856 is the first in which a Californian stands as a major-party nominee: **John C. Frémont** — the explorer of the Sierra crossings, a Bear Flag figure, and California's first U.S. Senator (1850–51, seated days after statehood) — runs as the new Republican Party's first presidential nominee. Democrat James Buchanan wins (174 electoral votes to Frémont's 114; Know-Nothing Millard Fillmore takes 8), but the date marks how fast Gold Rush California had arrived: six years after statehood, a Californian is on the national ticket. Sources: U.S. Senate, California Timeline (Frémont as first senator) · Wikipedia, 1856 United States presidential election (date, nominees, result) · checked Nov 4, 2026.

## California page
- **1924 — California legalizes professional boxing (Nov 4).** Voters pass a measure legalizing pro boxing in the state, outlawed since 1914 over safety concerns. The same day, Miriam "Ma" Ferguson (Texas) and Nellie Tayloe Ross (Wyoming) become the first women elected governor in the U.S. Source: HISTORY, This Day in History — Nov 4.
- **1952 — A Californian elected Vice President (Nov 4).** Sen. Richard M. Nixon of California is elected vice president on the Eisenhower ticket. Source: U.S. Senate, California Timeline.
- **1980 — A former California governor elected President (Nov 4).** Ronald Reagan, former governor of California, defeats President Jimmy Carter. Source: AP/HISTORY Nov 4 roundups.
- **2008 — Obama elected; California's Prop 8 passes (Nov 4).** Barack Obama is elected the 44th president, the first African American elected to the White House; the same day, California voters approve Proposition 8. Source: HISTORY, Nov 4.
