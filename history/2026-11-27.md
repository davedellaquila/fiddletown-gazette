# This Day in History — Friday, November 27, 2026

Placement: MAIN column, after The Week Ahead (`#history`). Set by the Morning Edition; Midday/Evening carry forward unchanged.

**Local check (honest):** the desk dug back to 1848 through Gold Country timelines, Amador County histories, and Mother Lode records and found no verified Fiddletown, Amador County, or Mother Lode event dated November 27. The gold panel says so plainly. The California layer does carry the date, hard: the 1978 assassinations of San Francisco Mayor George Moscone and Supervisor Harvey Milk — told straight, not softened.

**Items:**
- **1978 — Moscone and Milk assassinated in San Francisco (Nov 27)** — former Supervisor Dan White shoots Mayor George Moscone and Supervisor Harvey Milk — one of the first openly gay elected officials in the United States — inside San Francisco City Hall. A California date the whole country remembers.
- **1940 — Bruce Lee is born in San Francisco (Nov 27)** — the actor and martial artist is born at Jackson Street Hospital in San Francisco's Chinatown while his father, a Cantonese opera star, is on tour.
- **1895 — Alfred Nobel signs the will creating the Nobel Prizes (Nov 27)** — Nobel, the inventor of dynamite — a substance Gold Country miners knew well — leaves his fortune to fund prizes for those who "have conferred the greatest benefit to humankind."
- **1924 — The first Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade (Nov 27)** — Macy's employees march through New York with floats, bands, and animals from the Central Park Zoo; the giant balloons arrive a few years later. A fitting item for the day after Thanksgiving.
- **1942 — The French fleet scuttles itself at Toulon (Nov 27)** — as German forces move to seize it, the Vichy French navy sinks more than 70 of its own ships and submarines rather than let them fall into Nazi hands. Jimi Hendrix is born the same day in Seattle.
- **1868 — The Washita River attack (Nov 27)** — Lt. Col. George Armstrong Custer's 7th Cavalry attacks Black Kettle's Cheyenne village on the Washita River in present-day Oklahoma, killing Black Kettle and dozens of Cheyenne — a hard chapter of the Plains wars, named plainly.
- **1095 — Pope Urban II calls the First Crusade (Nov 27)** — speaking at the Council of Clermont in France, Urban II urges Western Christians to march east — the speech that launches the Crusades.
