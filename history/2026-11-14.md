## HIGHLIGHT — a reporter's day (gold panel)
- The desk dug back to 1848 for a Nov 14 event in Fiddletown or Amador County itself in the Gold Rush years — **none could be verified**. The closest local find in the record is a day off: Bayard Taylor walked into Drytown, Amador County's oldest town, on **November 15**, 1849, finding two to three hundred miners settled in for the winter — tomorrow's date, not today's, and the box does not stretch it. The box says so plainly.
- Highlight (national, and a newspaper's own): **1889 — New York World reporter Nellie Bly sets out to travel around the world in less than 80 days (Nov 14), 137 years ago today.** Elizabeth Cochrane, writing as Nellie Bly, left New York with a small handbag to beat the fictional record in Jules Verne's *Around the World in Eighty Days* — she finished in 72 days, 6 hours, a world record, and one of the great newspaper stunts that was also great reporting. ## National page
- **1851 — Herman Melville's *Moby-Dick; or, The Whale* is published in the United States (Nov 14)** by Harper & Brothers in New York, 175 years ago today — a month after its British release, to mixed reviews; it is now counted among the great American novels.
- **1883 — Robert Louis Stevenson's *Treasure Island* is first published as a book (Nov 14)** by Cassell & Co. — the pirate map, Long John Silver, and half the pirate lore in the language start here.
- **1960 — Ruby Bridges becomes the first Black child to attend an all-white elementary school in Louisiana (Nov 14).** Six-year-old Ruby Bridges, escorted by federal marshals, desegregated William Frantz Elementary School in New Orleans.
- **1969 — Apollo 12 launches (Nov 14)** — NASA's second crewed mission to the surface of the Moon; the Saturn V was struck by lightning twice shortly after liftoff and flew on.
