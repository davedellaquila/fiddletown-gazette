## HIGHLIGHT — Living history, tonight in our own hills (Dave's living-history-tie rule)
- **2026 — Gold Rush history takes the screen at a Gold Rush mine, tonight (Nov 7).** The documentary *Ghosts of the Gold Rush* (65 minutes, directed by California filmmaker Brandon Alvis) makes its world premiere tonight at the historic Kennedy Gold Mine in Jackson — gates 4:00 PM, screening 6:00 PM, cast-and-crew Q&A after, VIP paranormal investigation 8:00 PM–1:00 AM. The film examines the darker chapters of Gold Rush California — Hangtown's violent past, the 1922 Argonaut Mine disaster here in Jackson, a family murder connected to gold, and reported hauntings at historic landmarks — presented as witness accounts and investigative perspectives, not established fact. A living-history tie in the strictest sense: the beat's own history, screened at one of its own landmarks, on this date. Sources: InEDC, "Gold Rush Ghost Documentary Premieres at Kennedy Mine" (Nov 4, 2026; Kennedy Mine Foundation schedule) · Film Daily (Nov 2, 2026) · LodeDown / Visit Amador / Best of Amador calendar readings, Nov 6–7.

## Watershed / Sierra page
- **1846 — The Donner Party is snowbound in the Sierra (Nov 7 falls inside the storm).** Snow began again on the evening of Nov 4, 1846 — the start of an eight-day storm — and by Nov 7 the Donner Party was trapped at Truckee Lake (now Donner Lake), its food already dangerously low. Two years before gold was found at Coloma, on the emigrant road over the same Sierra our watershed drains from. Source: Wikipedia, Donner Party (storm of Nov 4–11, 1846), checked Nov 7, 2026.

## California page
- **1962 — Nixon's "last press conference" (Nov 7).** The day after losing the California governor's race to Pat Brown (election Nov 6, 1962), Richard Nixon tells reporters in Los Angeles: "You won't have Nixon to kick around anymore." (He was elected president six years later.) Sources: USA Today, Today in History — Nov 7 · Nov 7 timelines, checked Nov 7, 2026.

## National page — Nov 7
- **1916 — Jeannette Rankin becomes the first woman elected to Congress (Nov 7).** The Montana suffragist wins a seat in the U.S. House of Representatives — four years before the 19th Amendment. Source: HISTORY, This Day in History — November 7.
- **1944 — Franklin D. Roosevelt wins an unprecedented fourth term (Nov 7).** Source: Nov 7 timelines (Jagran Josh / HISTORY roundups), checked Nov 7, 2026.
- **2000 — An election too close to call (Nov 7).** The Bush–Gore presidential election ends the night in a statistical tie, decided weeks later in Florida; the same day, Hillary Clinton is elected to the U.S. Senate from New York, the first former first lady elected to public office. Source: HISTORY, November 7 · USA Today, Nov 7.
- **1665 — The London Gazette is first published (Nov 7)** — the oldest surviving English newspaper. A Gazette notes a Gazette. Source: Nov 7 timelines, checked Nov 7, 2026.
