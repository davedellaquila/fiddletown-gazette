## HIGHLIGHT — watershed / Mother Lode gateway, Gold Rush era
- **1852 — Sacramento's Great Conflagration (night of Nov 2).** Fire starting at Madame Lanas's millinery shop at J and 4th Streets burned more than 80 percent of the structures in Sacramento — a city built mostly of canvas and wood, and the embarcadero/supply gateway every Mother Lode camp, Fiddletown included, depended on. Damage was estimated at about six million dollars. Within a month, 761 structures had been rebuilt, many in brick with iron shutters; the Lady Adams Building is the only original building in Old Sacramento to survive the fire. Sources: Sacramento History Museum, This Day in History (November) · History of Sacramento, California.

## California page
- **1947 — The Spruce Goose flies (Nov 2)** at Long Beach: Howard Hughes pilots the Hughes H-4 Hercules, the enormous wooden flying boat, on its first and only flight, about a minute over Long Beach Harbor. Source: AP Today in History, Nov 2.
- **Nov 2 is Día de los Muertos (Day of the Dead)** — All Souls' Day in the calendar, the second day of the Nov 1–2 observance honoring adult loved ones who have died, marked across California with ofrendas and cemetery visits. Source: USA Today California guide to Día de los Muertos events, Oct 30, 2026.
